# Ontario Research Funding Analysis (2018–2024)

This project analyzes Ontario-funded research projects from **July 2018 to November 2024**, using data from the [Ontario Research Funding Summary – Current](https://data.ontario.ca/en/dataset/ontario-research-funding-summary-current). The analysis supports strategic decision-making by evaluating how public funding has been distributed across research fields, regions, and institutions—and its impact on innovation outcomes in the province.

---

## 🎯 Project Objective

To uncover trends in **funding allocation**, **research outcomes**, and **financial commitment** across sectors and geographies, enabling stakeholders to:
- Prioritize high-impact research fields
- Optimize future funding strategies
- Support underfunded innovation areas
- Promote effective public-private collaboration

## 📈 Key Findings

- **High-Impact Cities**: Ottawa and Guelph lead in research activity and funding. Continued support here can strengthen Ontario’s innovation leadership.
- **Health & Medical Research**: This sector consistently receives high funding and delivers strong outcomes, warranting sustained investment.
- **Underfunded Areas**: Fields like Biomedical Engineering, Manufacturing, and Civil Engineering show potential but remain underserved.
- **Trends Over Time**: Funding peaks align with strategic health and technology initiatives. More stable year-on-year allocations are needed for long-term impact.
- **Public-Private Partnerships**: Collaborations are underutilized but critical for commercialization and economic growth.

---

## ✅ Recommendations

- 📌 **Maintain funding** in top-performing cities like Ottawa and Guelph  
- 🩺 **Prioritize medical & health research** for innovation in healthcare  
- 🔬 **Support emerging sectors** like Biomedical & Manufacturing  
- 📊 **Adjust funding based on project performance & outcomes**  
- 🤝 **Encourage industry–academia partnerships** for greater impact
